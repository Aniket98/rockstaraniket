### What is a role in postgresql?

Official Links
|_ [Link 1](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/15/user-manag.html)

The official link says:
```
PostgreSQL manages database access permissions using the concept of roles. A role can be thought of as either a database user, or a group of database users, depending on how the role is set up.
Roles can own database objects (for example, tables and functions) and can assign privileges on those objects to other roles to control who has access to which objects. Furthermore, it is possible to grant membership in a role to another role, thus allowing the member role to use privileges assigned to another role.

The concept of roles subsumes the concepts of “users” and “groups”. In PostgreSQL versions before 8.1, users and groups were distinct kinds of entities, but now there are only roles. Any role can act as a user, a group, or both.
```

Normally, in our daily life, we like to think of the term 'ROLE' usually as Database Login User.
But in postgresql, a role can act as a database login user, and as a Group as well.

This can seem confusing at first. But the idea is actually very simple.

A group usually means a collection of multiple users (roles).
If I give any grant to GROUP, that gets passed on the members (users) of the group as well.

Now, if we put attention here, the below is usual image in our minds:

GROUP
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ A group name.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ Cannot login. (No login capability)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ Can acquire grants (privileges).
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ Collection of users (or I can say --> I can share the group grants to multiple users at same time.)

USER
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ A user name.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ Can login.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ Can acquire grants (privileges).

If we see here, there is only 1 main difference.
"A group can share its privileges to multiple users".

Basically, a group can share its privileges to other users as well.

If I give this capability to the user, to share its privileges to other users ON DEMAND, the user can act like a group as well.

Let's create this new concept of USER:

USER
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ A user name.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ Can login.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ Can acquire its own grants (privileges) + Can share its own grants to other users

What else left? Usually, we have the image in mind that a group cannot login.
If I give the ON DEMAND capability to the user to ENABLE or DISALBE Login feature, it becomes exact as GROUP.

Also, a name is just a name. Instead of saying group name or user name, I will simply say 'name.'

USER
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ A name.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ Can login + Cannot login (capability to enable/disable login feature)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ Can acquire its own grants (privileges) + Can share its own grants to other users

Now, this has all the features a USER and a GROUP can have.

A USER means login enabled.
Since, I have the capability to disable login, its better I call it by some other entity term, and I will call it as a general term "ROLE".

"ROLE"
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ A name.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ Can login + Cannot login (capability to enable/disable login feature)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;|_ Can acquire its own grants (privileges) + Can give its own grants to other users

Now, this role has OPTIONS to configure how it behaves in the system.

If you enable the LOGIN Feature, it is meant to act as a --> USER (an individual account).
If you disable the LOGIN Feature and share its privileges with other roles, it acts as a --> GROUP (a bundle of permissions/members).

So, the same ROLE can act as a USER and GROUP depending on the way you configure.

<span style="color: #ADEFD1; background-color: #00203F;">Aniket:</span> But what benefit it gives? It removed the line/boundary between user and group.

<span style="color: #FEE715; font-weight: bold; background-color: #101820;">Teacher:</span> Yes, it removed the boundary line and that's how it becomes more powerful.

Now, you can even do this:

Create a ROLE: Login Feature + Give its own privileges to other users.

See that? It's like a group which can login. It's like a Hybrid version of User and Group.

It gives me other benefit as well.
I do not need to create a separate group. I can simply have a USER (Role with Login Feature) and simply share its privileges to other USERS or GROUPS or Any Other Hybrid Version.

So, you can connect Anyone to Anyone in ANY WAY.

ROLE 1 <------> ROLE 2
(USER)&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(USER)
(GROUP)&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(GROUP)
(Hybrid)&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(Hybrid)

It's kind of giving you proper control by removing boundaries.
That's why its simple, but powerful as well.
